## 2. Audit-unit and endeavour model (foundation — all later numbers depend on it)

### 2.1 Definitions
- **Complex**: a curated grouping of 2+ Zotero records treated as one audit unit.
- **Singleton**: a one-record audit unit. It may be a genuine project, work, software output, or other output; one-record cardinality does not determine its intellectual or historical type.
- **Audit unit**: a complex or a singleton. This is the unit that receives an expected-artifact inventory and a Phase-4 summary.
- **Project**: a genuine named research/artefact endeavour (e.g. *Digital Ricoeur*, *Proof Designer*), identifiable by a distinct product lineage, not merely a shared topic. **Project/work describes an endeavour or output, not record cardinality.** Events, software, editorial endeavours, works, and other outputs retain their genuine identities whether assigned to a complex or a singleton.
- **Item roles**: `primary-paper`, `secondary-commentary`, `book`, `software`, `web-page`, `dataset`, `video`, `other`.
- Every item gets exactly one **primary assignment**: an EP audit-unit ID, or `unresolved`, or `non-project` (with reason); plus optional **cross-references** to other audit units.
- **Accounting invariant (machine-checked):** every item in the clustering population (§4.0) appears exactly once across {audit-unit primaries, unresolved, non-project}; cross-references reference existing audit-unit IDs. At Phase-1 close this is **28 records in 11 complexes + 129 records in 129 singletons + 4 non-project records = 161 records**. The 129 singletons comprise 16 anchored singletons in EP-001…EP-027 and 113 previously unanchored singletons. Thus **11 complexes + 129 singletons = 140 audit units** proceed to Phase 2.
- **Projects need not be software endeavours** (user ruling 2026-10-19, EP-010): an event or editorial/curatorial endeavour can be the project, with its publications as **textual sub-projects** — e.g. the 30th International Ludwig Wittgenstein Symposium is the project; its two proceedings volumes are textual sub-projects. *Container* is a structural role recorded by `part-of`, never an exclusion from project-hood — an artefact can be both (cf. DAPHNET source collections, EP-009). The operative dividing line is artefact-of-an-endeavour vs record-about-an-endeavour.

### 2.2 Expected-artifact inventory (per audit unit)
- Each of the **140 audit units** carries an explicit **inventory of expected artifacts**, each an artifact ID whose machine proposal may be `required | optional | unknown` (e.g. `EP-017/paper/main [required]`, `EP-017/paper/followup [optional]`, `EP-017/software/repo [required]`). Machine-proposed from item roles after Phase 1; **confirmed/corrected at the Phase-2 human gate** (not the Phase-1 gate — Phase 1 decides grouping only).
- `sources/emerging_philosophy/decisions.jsonl` is authoritative for inventory rulings as well as gate rulings. Append `{"type":"inventory","id":"EP-028/software/repo","requirement":"required|optional|not-expected",...}`; the last record wins for each artifact ID. `required` and `optional` produce `proposed: false` and preserve `basis` plus optional `date`, `note`, and `evidence`; `not-expected` removes the row. New artifacts require an explicit `basis`. The YAML and Markdown review sheets are regenerated derived views, never direct editing surfaces. On 2026-10-26 the user approved 357 rulings, yielding 194 confirmed artifacts with no unknowns. An explicitly empty confirmed inventory is valid.
- Coverage denominators are computed **only** from confirmed `required` artifact IDs. Audit units with any `unknown` inventory entries are reported separately, never folded into coverage percentages. Coverage accounts for every complex and singleton, including one-record units with zero required artifacts.

### 2.3 Edge evidence types (for complex grouping)
`related-link` (existing Zotero Related — semantics unverified), `shared-tag`, `title-overlap`, `shared-identifier`, `creator-year`, `container` (**structural**: the user's bookSection `bookTitle`↔`book` title convention, plus chapter cliques = same-volume context; recorded but **never grouping-eligible**). All non-`container` edges are proposals. At the human gate each edge is resolved as `same-project | describes | implements | cites | part-of | unrelated`. **The legacy serialized token `same-project` operationally means `same complex`; only confirmed `same-project` edges form the connected components that define complexes.** `describes`/`implements`/`cites` edges are recorded as cross-references and never drive grouping (they may exist within or across audit units); `part-of` records containment (chapter↔volume) as structure and likewise never drives grouping. `contained-in-book` edges come pre-typed `part-of` by the convention and are corrected, not re-derived, at the gate. **Venue-ownership rule (gate convention, user ruling 2026-10-19):** an item in a venue controlled by the project's own authors (their own book, site, README) is the project's own output → `same-project`; a contribution to a venue controlled by others — a journal-article in an issue, a section/chapter in an edited book — is a description → `describes`, even when developer-authored and product-specific. Companion books with the same authors as the software are own-venue → `same-project`. **Refinement (user ruling 2026-10-19, EP-009):** *venue* means the intellectual forum (the journal, the conference, the book, the site) and *ownership* means editorial control of that forum — the publishing house and its stable of publications are orthogonal (Elsevier/Springer/CUP merely print). A monograph's venue is the book itself (author-controlled) regardless of press; a journal article is one of many in an issue under that journal's editors; a conference paper is one of many proceedings contributions under that conference's programme committee → `describes`. Carve-out: in the rare case a software project creates its *own* conference/workshop to publicise or showcase that particular project, contributions there are own-venue → `same-project`.

### 2.4 Identifiers
- **EP audit-unit IDs and artifact IDs are opaque and stable**. An EP ID is `EP-` followed by at least three digits (`EP-001`, `EP-1000`), assigned once and never recomputed from titles. EP-001…EP-027 remain the existing anchored IDs — now 11 complexes and 16 anchored singletons — and are **not project IDs**. Singleton IDs EP-028…EP-140 are held in `sources/emerging_philosophy/singleton-ids.json`, coordinated with anchored allocation. The derived sheet and singleton registry associate IDs with item keys; titles are display-only, re-rendered from the snapshot.
- Anchoring the same singleton record preserves its EP ID: its registry mapping becomes dormant while anchored and active again on return to singleton status. Existing mappings are retained during departure/dormancy. New IDs exceed the high-water mark across every active, dormant, logged, and retired ID; **no new allocation reuses an ID**. The existing active/retired EP-004, EP-020, and EP-022 history is an inherited pre-policy exception set, retained explicitly with version-qualified mappings; do not renumber it.
- **Merges:** the merged audit unit keeps the numerically lowest existing ID; all other IDs are retired. **Splits:** every resulting fragment receives a new ID; the original ID is retired. Retirements are appended to the derived sheet's `id-log` (ID, date, reason, successor ID(s)) and retained across regeneration.
- Legacy serialized `type: project` anchors an audit unit, `to_project` points to an EP audit-unit ID, and `non-project` excludes a record from the audit-unit population. These tokens remain unchanged for compatibility; their operative meanings do not conflate audit-unit cardinality with genuine project-hood.

## 4. Phase 1 — Complex grouping and singleton assignment

**4.0 Population.** The clustering population is exactly the snapshot's top-level items (non-attachment, non-note; 161 at baseline). Attachments and notes attach to audit units through their parent's assignment. Standalone or broken-ref attachments/notes (per §3e) are **orphans**: excluded from grouping, listed in an orphan register, surfaced at the human gate. Any future re-snapshot or reconciliation walk excludes the **staging subtree** (`MNG9HZDC` and any descendants) from the population — staging is a quarantine area whose items are reconciled only at Phase 5 (see execution log, 2026-10-19).

1. Build the candidate-edge graph over the population using §2.3 evidence. Calibrate against known positives: Digital Ricoeur, Proof Designer, PDC ontology, History of Philosophy.
2. **Output:** derived YAML and Markdown review views: proposed complexes with member keys, stable EP audit-unit IDs (§2.4), item roles, typed edges with confidence; singletons retained under the legacy label `unpaired — confirm`; `NEEDS-YOU` on ambiguity; orphan register appended. `decisions.jsonl` is authoritative for gate and inventory rulings; neither generated view is edited directly.
3. **Human gate (grouping only):** record each edge ruling (`same-project | describes | implements | cites | unrelated`) and assignment ruling (splits/merges/singleton confirmations) in the ledger. ## 5. Phase 2 — Source-material audit per audit unit

Phase 2 covers **all 140 audit units: 11 complexes and 129 singletons**. For each **expected inventory artifact** (§2.2), classify with route and confidence:

- **Software:** git host → `clonable`; homepage archive → `downloadable`; dead link with Wayback hits → `wayback`; none → `lost-candidate`.
- **Web/video/dataset:** Wayback snapshot reference or `reference-only`.
- **Output:** audit table (CSV + readable): artifact ID → status, route, evidence, confidence, with every audit unit represented.
- **Human gate:** confirm/correct each audit unit's expected-artifact inventory by appending §2.2 `inventory` rulings to `decisions.jsonl` — resolve every `unknown`, and use `not-expected` to remove absent artifacts. Do not edit generated YAML. Then review `manual`, `unresolved`, `lost-candidate` rows.
- **Verify:** audit rows == each §2.2 inventory exactly (same artifact IDs); every status carries evidence (URL, HTTP code, or attachment key); all 140 audit units have been considered. Verifier checks 1–7 always run and include exact snapshot population keys, all `to_project` targets, and inventory structure/rulings in addition to the gate, accounting, and ID invariants. ## 6. Phase 3 — Acquisition

- Artifact IDs per §2.4 (multiple papers per audit unit are distinct artifacts).
- Order: Zotero stored PDFs → Unpaywall/OpenAlex → direct URLs → git clones → homepage downloads → Wayback. Serial, polite delays, identifying User-Agent, bounded retries (3, backoff).
- Per-artifact `manifest.json`: source URL, fetched-at, HTTP status, provenance, attempt log (failures included), verification fields. Status ∈ `acquired | failed | manual | reference-only | absent-expected`.
- **Verification per artifact type:** files (PDFs etc.): sha256 + size + `%PDF` magic where applicable (+`pdfinfo` when available). **Repositories separately:** a directory has no single checksum — record remote URL, `rev-parse HEAD`, shallow/full, clone exit status, `git fsck` result.
- Resumption: skip only `acquired` artifacts whose on-disk verification still passes — files: sha256 match; **repos: `rev-parse HEAD` match AND `git fsck` clean AND `git status --porcelain` empty** (fsck alone misses edited/deleted checked-out files); all others re-attempt or await human input.
- **Verify:** terminal-state rule — every planned artifact has a recorded terminal outcome; each file/clone on disk belongs to exactly one manifest.

## 7. Phase 4 — Audit-unit summaries

Phase 4 covers **all 140 audit units**, both complexes and singletons.

- Per audit unit, one structured page: name, decade, people and other agentive entities, description, artifacts w/ local paths, liveness (`alive | dead | link-rotted | lost | unknown` — acquisition failure alone never implies `dead`/`lost`), significance *(marked as interpretation)*, open questions. Identify the genuine project/work/output where applicable without inferring it from singleton or complex cardinality.
- LLM-assisted drafting from bib data + notes + acquired full text/READMEs. Every factual claim pinned to a source (path or URL); low-confidence flagged `[?]`.
- **Two-tier audit:** (a) automatic — 100% of pinned targets checked to exist; (b) human — stratified-by-decade sample of **min(N, max(10, ceil(0.1·N)))** summaries reviewed for claim support.
- Output: per-audit-unit `summary.md` + compiled **`AUDIT_UNITS.md`** index covering all 140 audit units.
- **Verify:** tier (a) passes fully; tier (b) defects corrected before Phase 5; the index accounts for every complex and singleton.
